# Review: dossiers/sp500.md (round 1 of 3)

**Reviewer:** compliance/review agent · **Date:** 2026-09-19
**Verdict: FIX**

This is a well-sourced draft with unusually careful citation discipline — of roughly 20 quoted sources spot-checked independently (see below), every quote I checked matched the live source exactly, word for word. That is the opposite of what I usually find. But there are real gaps: one internal inconsistency that undercuts the dossier's own central argument (VUSA's distribution type), one cost-stack gap that is arguably a "blank line" under methodology §3, and one source-tier judgment call that needs Lucas's sign-off because it's the sole support for a headline number. None of these are fabrications; all are fixable without new research trips.

## Findings

### 1. [Major] VUSA is a Distributing share class, not Accumulating — the dossier never says so, despite building its central argument on exactly this distinction

Section 1.1 spends several paragraphs establishing that the Acc/Dist distinction is the crux of why total-return (not price-return) is the right benchmark: "An accumulation unit... reinvests dividends automatically inside the fund... a holder who spends the cash distributions from an income unit... does not [capture dividends]."

Then section 2.1 names **VUSA** (Vanguard S&P 500 UCITS ETF) as the flagship Route A example, explicitly tags **CSPX** as "(accumulating)," but says nothing about VUSA's distribution type. I checked this independently: VUSA is the ticker for the **Distributing** share class (Vanguard S&P 500 UCITS ETF (USD) **Distributing**, ISIN IE00B3XXRP09); the accumulating equivalent is a different fund/ticker (VUAG, IE00BFMXXD54). Section 3.4 then lumps VUSA and CSPX together under "GBP-denominated share classes/listings (e.g. VUSA and CSPX's GBP line on the LSE)" and separately states "Accumulating share classes... never generate a cash dividend for the investor to convert at all" without clarifying that this does *not* describe VUSA.

A reader who takes the dossier's own logic seriously (Acc vs Dist matters for whether total return is actually captured) and then reads "VUSA" repeatedly alongside "CSPX (accumulating)" without a corrective note will reasonably assume VUSA behaves the same way. It doesn't — a VUSA holder who spends the distributions is, by the dossier's own definition, closer to holding the price-return series economically, not the total-return series the company's methodology mandates as the benchmark.

**Fix:** Add an explicit line in section 2.1 (or a footnote wherever VUSA is named) stating VUSA is the Distributing share class, and either name the accumulating equivalent (VUAG) as the more representative example for this company's total-return-based methodology, or explain why VUSA is used anyway.

### 2. [Major] Bid-ask spread for the ETF route is a real, applicable cost left blank in both Buying and Selling — arguably violates methodology §3's "blank line blocks publication" rule

Methodology §3: "Every asset write-up fills in all of these lines. If a cost does not apply, write 'none' and say why. **A blank line blocks publication.**"

Section 3.1's "Dealer/market spread (bid–ask on exchange)" row for the ETF route has no number in Low, Central, or High — it says "Not independently quoted in this pass" in all three columns. Section 3.3 (Selling) treats it the same way. This is not a cost that "does not apply" — the dossier itself confirms it applies ("the bid-ask spread from 3.1 is the equivalent [selling] cost, unquantified in this pass"). It is a cost stack line that genuinely applies to the flagship product (VUSA/CSPX) and has no figure.

This matters because it is not a trivial or theoretical cost — bid-ask spread is one of the most commonly cited "hidden" costs of ETF investing, and its omission means any future NRR calculation built on this dossier's cost stack would be missing a real cost of buying and selling the ETF route entirely, with no number to substitute even at the "high" end.

**Fix:** Either (a) pull a live indicative spread from the LSE order book or a data vendor for VUSA/CSPX before this dossier is treated as complete, or (b) if genuinely unobtainable in this pass, state explicitly in the cost-stack table itself (not just in a footnote) that this line is incomplete and that the dossier is not yet publication-ready for a cost-scenario calculation until it is filled — rather than leaving three dashes that look, at a skim, like "not applicable."

### 3. [Moderate — needs Lucas's/Compliance's call] RBC Wealth Management is the *sole* source for the headline "nearly 41 percent" concentration figure, and its Tier B classification is a judgment call that should be made explicitly, not assumed

House rules B5: "No Tier C source is the only support for any number." Methodology §7.1: "A source written by someone selling the asset (a dealer, a platform, a tool vendor) is at most Tier C, unless it publishes its underlying data and method."

I fetched the RBC page directly. It confirms the quotes in the dossier are accurate (see verification below), and confirms RBC Wealth Management's own site describes itself as offering investment advisory and wealth management services generally ("a division of RBC Capital Markets, LLC, registered investment adviser"), with a standard disclaimer that its "[i]nvestment and insurance products... are subject to investment risks, including possible loss of the principal amount invested." The dossier's own reasoning for Tier B — "RBC is a seller of investment advice generally, not specifically a seller of S&P 500 tracker products" — draws a distinction the methodology text doesn't explicitly make (the rule says "someone selling the asset," and RBC does sell equity-market advisory services that a "concentration risk" narrative plausibly supports commercially). The methodology's carve-out ("unless it publishes its underlying data and method") is met here (RBC discloses FactSet as the data source and the 12/31/25 as-of date), which is a defensible basis for Tier B — but it's exactly the kind of edge case the methodology's tier rules were written to catch, and it's the *only* source behind a striking, headline-grade number ("41 percent," more than double the 2015 figure). This should go to Lucas as a explicit tier-confirmation, not be settled silently by the researcher's own footnote.

**Fix:** Flag explicitly (not just in a footnote) for Lucas: "is RBC Wealth Management's disclosed-methodology research Tier B, given it is a wealth manager, and is it acceptable as the sole source for this headline number?" If the answer is no, the 41% figure needs a second, independent corroborating source (S&P DJI's own figure would be the strongest, but that's blocked — see Finding 6) before it can be published as a headline claim.

### 4. [Minor] Source-tier list formatting: SEC filings placed under the "Tier A" heading in section 9 while the text says to treat them as Tier B

Section 9's "Tier A" bullet list includes: "SEC EDGAR filings (...) — treated as Tier B below given they are fund-specific documents rather than official statistics, but flagged as A/B-adjacent given their regulatory-filed status." This sentence contradicts its own placement — it's listed under the Tier A heading while saying it should be under Tier B. A downstream reader (or the Compliance agent doing a B5 check) skimming the section headers would misread these as Tier A.

**Fix:** Move this line to the Tier B list, or split it cleanly: state once, clearly, "SEC filings are Tier B in this dossier" and place it there.

### 5. [Minor] FCA clone-firm press release is presented without its original publication date, and it is five years old

I fetched the release directly: it is dated **27 January 2021**, not something newly published. The dossier cites it as "fetched directly 2026-09-19" (true) but never states the release's own date, so a reader would reasonably assume it reflects a 2026 warning rather than 2021 guidance being reused. The underlying definition of a "clone firm" is evergreen and the quotes are accurate (verified independently, word for word), so this isn't a factual problem — but the citation rule (§7.3) is about giving readers enough information to judge a source, and silently omitting a five-year-old publication date on a "current risk" claim is a small transparency gap.

**Fix:** Add the release's own date ("FCA press release, 27 January 2021, still live on fca.org.uk as of 2026-09-19") to the citation.

### 6. [Minor] Dilution levy (a real selling-side cost for some open-ended funds) has no row in the Selling cost stack (3.3)

Section 3.5 mentions, almost as an aside, that "a 'dilution levy' can apply on large flows in some funds; not confirmed for Fidelity Index US Fund specifically." This is a genuine, named selling-side cost category (comparable to a "dealer buy-back spread" for the fund route) that methodology §3's Selling row list would expect to see addressed in section 3.3, not buried in the time-to-sell table.

**Fix:** Add a line to 3.3's Route B costs (or a note against "Dealer buy-back spread") explicitly addressing the dilution levy, even if the answer is "not confirmed for Fidelity Index US Fund; flagged as an open question."

## Overall assessment

The tax section (§4) is the strongest part of this dossier — directly sourced to HMRC/gov.uk throughout, dates stated, quotes verified word-for-word against live pages. The stamp duty/SDRT sourcing (§3.1) is genuinely excellent (UK statutory instruments, not secondary commentary). The citation discipline required by methodology §7.3 has clearly been followed in the vast majority of cases I checked.

The reason this is **Fix** rather than **Pass**: Finding 1 (VUSA's distribution type) is a real internal-consistency problem that undermines the dossier's own central methodological argument about total return, Finding 2 (the unquantified ETF spread) is arguably a blank-line violation of methodology §3, and Finding 3 (RBC's tier) needs an explicit decision from Lucas rather than being settled unilaterally, since it's the sole support for a striking headline number. None of these require a fresh research pass from scratch — they're targeted fixes.

The two escalations (S&P DJI access, and total-return licensing) are correctly identified as genuinely unresolved; I hit the same walls independently and found supporting evidence that the licensing question is substantive, not a formality. Both should go to Lucas as flagged, unchanged.
