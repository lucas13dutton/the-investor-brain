# Data licensing research: can we buy a licensed daily price/NAV series for the S&P 500 benchmark?

**Status:** research note, not a decision · **Owner:** researcher agent · **Date checked:** 2026-09-19
Built per `docs/methodology.md` (v0.8) and `docs/house-rules.md`. Where this note and those documents disagree, the documents win.

## Why this note exists

`dossiers/sp500.md` (section 1.1, section 5, Open Questions §8.10) and `dossiers/gold.md` both hit the same wall: the official index/benchmark provider (S&P Dow Jones Indices for the S&P 500; LBMA/ICE for gold) requires a paid commercial licence, and the fund issuers whose products this company actually benchmarks against (iShares CSPX, Vanguard VUSA/VUAG, Fidelity Index US Fund) don't offer a confirmed, downloadable, multi-year daily NAV history on their own retail websites — and even where they do, their website terms broadly restrict "derivative works."

This note asks a narrower, practical question: **is there a market-data vendor that (a) actually holds the daily price/NAV history we need, far enough back, and (b) whose licence terms explicitly say we may calculate a derived figure (a cost- and inflation-adjusted return statistic) from their data and publish that figure to a commercial audience** — not just use the raw data internally, not just display it as-is?

Four vendors were checked: **EODHD, Twelve Data, Polygon.io (rebranded Massive.com, 30 Oct 2025), and Financial Modeling Prep (FMP)**. All four were reachable to some degree; FMP's own website blocked every direct-fetch attempt made in this pass (see its row below) so its entry is weaker than the other three and is flagged as such rather than treated as equivalent evidence.

**Sourcing note on tiers:** per house rules, a vendor's own marketing or pricing page describing its own product is **Tier C** — background only, never sufficient on its own to support a number, because the vendor is the seller of what's being described. A vendor's own published **Terms of Service**, by contrast, is treated here as the **primary document for what the contract actually says** (there is no other source for that) — but it is still the seller's own document, drafted to protect the seller, so where it is ambiguous this note says so plainly rather than reading it generously. No number or licence conclusion below rests on a Tier C marketing claim alone.

## What this shows, read across all four vendors

Every vendor whose terms could actually be read in this pass — EODHD, Twelve Data, and Polygon.io/Massive — draws the same basic line, worded differently:

- Ordinary use of the data (building your own charts, running your own analysis) is fine under a standard paid plan.
- **Publishing something built from that data to people outside your own organisation is treated as a separate, more restricted thing** — at best gated behind an "add-on" or "separate written agreement" (Twelve Data), at worst banned outright without a specific licence naming "index," "net asset value" or "derivative works" (Polygon.io/Massive).
- None of the three readable vendors' published, self-serve pricing tiers come with a clause that plainly says: *"yes, you may calculate a return figure from our data and publish that figure to your paying customers or general audience."* Where the door is open at all (Twelve Data's Derived Data definition), it sits next to another clause that seems to require a separate, unpriced conversation before you can actually publish anything built from it.

This matches the pattern this company's own `dossiers/sp500.md` and `dossiers/gold.md` already found with S&P Dow Jones Indices, LBMA/ICE, iShares and Fidelity: **every source close to "the real number" treats redistribution and derivative-works rights as a separate, negotiated thing from basic data access**, not something a published price list settles on its own.

## Recommendation

**No vendor checked in this pass can be recommended for a subscription today**, on the evidence gathered:

1. **Twelve Data** is the strongest lead: it is the only vendor that directly confirmed holding the exact UK tickers this company already uses (CSPX and VUSA, matched to the LSE/GBP listing used in `dossiers/sp500.md`), and its Terms of Use are the only ones that name a "Derived Data" concept that sounds like what a Net Real Return calculation actually is. But its own Terms do not clearly say whether publishing that Derived Data to a public or paid audience needs the separate "Redistribution Rights Add-On" — and that add-on has no published price, meaning the real cost of a workable Twelve Data licence is unknown until Twelve Data's sales team answers a direct question.
2. **EODHD** is plausible on coverage (confirmed LSE access; S&P 500 index ticker referenced in its own documentation, though not confirmed on a pricing page) but equally unclear on licensing, and — like Twelve Data — points unresolved cases to its sales team rather than answering in its published terms.
3. **Polygon.io/Massive** should be ruled out now: it has no UK exchange coverage at all, so it cannot supply the ETF/NAV side of what's needed regardless of price, and separately, its own terms are the most explicit and specific prohibition on exactly this use found anywhere in this research (naming "index," "net asset value" and "investment strategy" as things you may not derive from its data without a specific licence).
4. **Financial Modeling Prep** could not be assessed at all in this pass — its own website blocked every automated access attempt made. It should not be ruled in or out; it needs a human to open the site directly, or a direct email to FMP.

**If this company wants to pursue this further**, the concrete, low-cost next step is not to subscribe to anything yet. It is to send the same precise, written question to Twelve Data's and EODHD's sales/support teams (and, separately, to FMP once its site can actually be read) along the lines of: *"We want to calculate a single derived statistic — a cost- and inflation-adjusted annual return figure — from your licensed NAV/price data for [named funds/tickers], and publish that one number, with full sourcing, in commercial video, newsletter and app content. Does your [plan name] licence permit this, or does it require your Redistribution Rights Add-On / a separate agreement, and if so, what does that cost?"* A written answer to that question, from the vendor itself, would settle in one email what several hours of reading standard-form website terms could not.

## Escalation to Lucas

This note deliberately does **not** recommend committing to spend, per the task and per methodology's/house-rules' instruction that a data source requiring payment or a licence agreement is escalated, not decided by an agent.

**What needs Lucas's sign-off before anything happens:**

- **Any paid subscription to any of these vendors** (or a fifth vendor not checked here) — this is a recurring cost decision, and per the pattern found above, the lowest tier that superficially "covers the data" is very unlikely to be the lowest tier that actually, legally, covers what this company wants to do with it. The real cost may only become clear after a sales conversation, and could be materially higher than the published self-serve price if a custom redistribution agreement is required.
- **Any direct written question sent to a vendor's sales team** describing this company's business (an AI-agent-run investment research/education company publishing commercial content) — this is effectively an early-stage commercial negotiation and a public statement about the business, which this note treats as Lucas's call, not something to send unprompted.
- **A decision to proceed without a vendor's explicit written yes** — i.e., relying on an ambiguous reading of Twelve Data's or EODHD's terms (as opposed to Polygon.io/Massive's, which is unambiguous and negative) — carries real legal and reputational risk for a commercial publisher and should not be a call this research note or any single agent makes alone.

**What this note is not saying:** it is not saying no honest cost model or licensed data path exists at all — Twelve Data and EODHD remain live possibilities pending a direct answer. It is saying that, as things stand, this company does not yet have a confirmed, written, affordable, legally clear route to a licensed multi-year daily price/NAV series it can build derived published figures from — the same conclusion `dossiers/sp500.md` already reached for S&P Dow Jones Indices, iShares and Fidelity directly.

5. **Whether any of these vendors' NAV/price data for an accumulating share class (e.g. CSPX) already reflects total return** (since accumulating funds reinvest dividends inside the NAV) **or whether a separate total-return adjustment would still be needed** — not checked in this pass; relevant to whether this data, once licensed, would need combining with anything else to match this company's benchmark definition (methodology §6).

## Confidence: Low

**What's solid:** three vendors' own published terms and pricing pages were read directly and quoted verbatim with access dates (EODHD, Twelve Data, Polygon.io/Massive) — this meets the citation bar in methodology §7.3. Twelve Data's coverage of the two named UK ETF tickers was confirmed with a live, direct API query, not just a marketing claim. Polygon.io/Massive's lack of UK coverage, and its explicit ban on deriving an "index" or "net asset value" from its data, are both clearly and directly sourced, so that vendor can be confidently ruled out.

**What holds this back from Medium or High:** the single most important question — will any vendor's actual, priced plan let this company calculate and publish a derived Net Real Return figure to a commercial audience — could not be answered with confidence for any of the four vendors. Two (EODHD, Twelve Data) are genuinely ambiguous on their own published wording; one (FMP) could not be read at all; only one (Polygon.io/Massive) gave a clear answer, and it was "no, not without a separate licence." No vendor's sales team was actually contacted in this pass (that step is escalated to Lucas above, not taken unilaterally). Historical depth for the specific UK ETF tickers needed was not confirmed for any vendor.

**What would raise it:** a written answer from Twelve Data's and EODHD's sales/support teams to the specific question in the Recommendation section above; a successful direct read of FMP's own terms of service and pricing page (by a human browser, or once automated access stops being blocked); and, for whichever vendor looks most promising after that, a trial-account check of actual historical depth for CSPX, VUSA, VUAG and Fidelity Index US Fund specifically, before any subscription is purchased.
