## Verdict: **Fix** (scoped to these 10 rows)

Not a clean Pass: one row (GOLD-0007) carries an `EXCEPTION APPROVED` marker whose own stated justification is factually false for that row, and one row (GOLD-0056) states a broader claim than the dossier actually established. Neither is a fabricated number in the sense of "invented the figure" — both underlying values are plausible and the dossier's own text is honest about the gap — but the **evidence-log row itself** overstates what's been verified, which is exactly the kind of drift the log exists to prevent. Not Block, because 8 of 10 rows check out cleanly, including both correctly-left-failing rows and one correctly-applied exception, and the two problems found are fixable by editing text, not by redoing research. This does, however, raise a real question about how many of the other 60 exception-marked rows share GOLD-0007's defect — see Finding 1 and "What I couldn't check."

## Findings

### 1. [Critical] GOLD-0007 (and its twin GOLD-0008): the `EXCEPTION APPROVED` marker's own justification is false for this row

**Claim:** "Buyer's premium when buying physical gold direct from a dealer or the Royal Mint (the ordinary retail channel) is 0% (low scenario)."

**`calculation_ref` field states:** "EXCEPTION APPROVED by Lucas, 2026-09-19 — the seller/platform's own current, directly-fetched, dated fee or price page, with no Tier A or B alternative available for this figure."

That is not what this row is. `source_1_name` is "Standard bullion dealer/Mint direct-purchase practice"; `source_1_url` is literally "n/a — structural fact, not a cited page"; `source_1_quote` says outright: "No verbatim source cited; this is a structural fact about the ordinary buying channel, not an individually sourced figure." There is no seller, no platform, no page, no fetch, and no date attached to an actual document — the 0% figure is the researcher's own logical inference ("no auction house is involved, so there's no buyer's-premium concept"), which may well be correct, but it is not "the seller/platform's own current, directly-fetched, dated fee or price page" by any reading. Cross-checked against `dossiers/gold.md` §3.1: the table's Source cell for the Buyer's premium row is dominated by Noonans Mayfair's citation (which supports the *high*-scenario 24% figure only); the low/central 0% cells carry no citation of their own in the dossier either.

Applying the exception marker here doesn't just miscategorise a Tier C source — it manufactures a false paper trail for what the exception was actually approved for. The brief for this task states the reasoning for *withholding* the marker from genuinely unsourced rows was "granting it would mean fabricating Lucas's approval for claims that don't actually meet the stated bar." This row does exactly that in reverse: it grants the marker to a row that plainly doesn't meet the bar the marker itself states.

**Same defect, not individually verified in depth but visible on the same read-through:** GOLD-0008 (identical claim, central scenario, identical non-source), GOLD-0014 (home storage £0, "Standard home-storage practice," "n/a — structural fact"), GOLD-0035 (postage £0 for in-person sale, same pattern), GOLD-0047 (ETC commission £0, "Standard platform free-trade-allowance practice"), GOLD-0051 (ETC FX fee 0%, "Standard exchange-trading mechanics"), GOLD-0062 (ETC platform fee 0%, "Standard fee-free share account practice") — all six carry `source_1_url` = "n/a — structural fact, not a cited page" **and** the `EXCEPTION APPROVED` marker. GOLD-0090 (Action Fraud transition "completed December 2025") also carries the marker despite (a) not being a fee/price claim at all — it's a government service-transition date — and (b) being a claim the cited skeptic review already found imprecise ("Finding 4 — Minor... 'completed December 2025' overstates how finished the transition was. Not corrected in dossiers/gold.md as of this pass"). Granting a sourcing exception to a claim already flagged as inaccurate, uncorrected, is worse than granting it to a merely-unsourced one.

**What would fix it:** Either (a) stop using the `EXCEPTION APPROVED` marker for rows that are structural/logical inferences with no actual page behind them — these need a different marker or a different validator rule (e.g. "structural fact, not a cost claim, exempted from tiering") — or (b) if Lucas's approval genuinely was meant to cover these too, rewrite the `calculation_ref` text so it doesn't claim a seller/platform page exists when it doesn't. Right now the text is simply inaccurate about its own row.

### 2. [Major] GOLD-0056 (and GOLD-0057, GOLD-0058): claim generalises past what the dossier actually verified

**Claim:** "Stamp Duty Reserve Tax (SDRT) on UK-listed gold ETCs is 0% (low/central/high scenario, ETC buying)."

The Tier A source and quote are accurately transcribed from `dossiers/gold.md` §4.3 (STSM031090, "issued or raised by a company incorporated outside the UK (and with no UK register)"), and the `checked_by` note accurately reflects `reviews/gold-review-2026-09-18-v2.md`'s Finding 1 ("Fixed, confirmed... cites the correct manual page and quotes it correctly"). Both of those check out.

The problem is scope. The dossier is explicit that this SDRT-exemption reasoning was verified for **one issuer only**: "This has only been verified for the iShares issuer; Invesco, WisdomTree and Royal Mint's ETC issuers were not individually checked for incorporation in this pass — see Open Questions §8.6" (dossier §4.3), and Open Question item 5 repeats this as unresolved. The evidence-log claim text, though, asserts the 0% figure for "UK-listed gold ETCs" as a class — plural, general, no caveat. A reader of the evidence log alone (which is meant to be citable on its own, per the README: "If you're checking whether a specific published figure is backed by evidence, look it up by `claim_id` here first") would have no way to know this was only confirmed for SGLN.

**What would fix it:** Either scope the claim text to "iShares Physical Gold ETC (SGLN)" specifically, matching what was actually verified, or add the same caveat the dossier carries ("verified for one issuer; not independently confirmed for other UK-listed gold ETC issuers").

### 3. [Moderate] GOLD-0087: Tier A assigned to content that was never actually read from its primary source

**Claim:** "A City of London Police boiler-room fraud case involved £12m of fraud, for which the convicted fraudster's Rolls Royce Wraith and gold bars were seized."

The quote is accurately transcribed and matches `dossiers/gold.md` §7 verbatim. But both the dossier and the evidence row are honest that this is "reached via search indexing" because "direct fetch of cityoflondon.police.uk was blocked, 403." The row and dossier both tier this as **A** on the reasoning that it's an "official police source." That reasoning conflates the *authority* of the underlying institution with the *reliability of the specific text reaching this dossier* — nobody (researcher or either skeptic review) has actually read the live press release; the wording comes from a search engine's cached title/snippet, which can be truncated, stale, or mismatched to the live page. Methodology's Tier A definition is "official statistics, or licensed actual sale prices" — a title string surfaced by a search index, of a page nobody could open, is a weaker evidentiary chain than that definition implies, even though the ultimate source (the police force) would be Tier A if actually read. This is used only as risk/scam colour, not a headline NRR number, so the practical stakes are low, but the tier label overstates the actual verification chain.

**What would fix it:** Either get a human to open the live page and confirm the quote, or tier this frankly as "Tier A source, unverified quote (reached via search index only)" rather than unqualified Tier A.

## What I couldn't check

- **The other 133 rows.** Findings 1 and 2 surfaced from a full read-through of the CSV done to select a representative sample, not from formal deep-checking of every row — I flagged the pattern (structural-fact rows carrying the "seller/platform... fee or price page" exception text) because it was visible on that read-through, but I did not systematically verify all 61 exception-marked rows against their dossier source. **This needs a full pass**, not a 10-row sample, to know how many of the 61 exception grants share GOLD-0007's defect.
- **Whether Lucas's actual approval on 2026-09-19 was a blanket "approve every Tier-C-only row the researcher flags" or a row-by-row review.** If it was row-by-row, GOLD-0007/0008/0014/0035/0047/0051/0062/0090 should not have qualified and the marker text is simply wrong about what was approved. If it was a blanket policy decision applied mechanically after the fact by whoever wrote the marker text, the marker's own wording ("the seller/platform's own current, directly-fetched, dated fee or price page") is still inaccurate for these specific rows regardless of who approved what. Either way this needs Lucas to confirm which it was — **escalating this, per the escalation rule for findings suggesting a process error, not just a content error.**
- **Live re-fetch of any primary source myself.** I checked evidence-row text against dossier text and against the cited skeptic reviews' text, and checked the dossier's own account of what it fetched — I did not independently re-fetch HMRC, gov.uk, FCA, eBay, Noonans, or GSAM pages myself. This mirrors what the two skeptic reviews already did (and did well, per their own "what checked out cleanly" sections), so I relied on their independent verification rather than repeating it.
- **`approved_by` and `status` columns.** All 143 rows are `status: draft` with `approved_by` blank, so none of this is live-published yet regardless of the above findings — there is no immediate public-facing correction needed. This review is pre-publication quality control, not a live-corrections matter.
