## Findings

### 1. [Critical] Six numeric claims appear in the dossier with no bracketed claim ID and no matching evidence.csv row

The dossier states in its own preamble (line 6): "Every numeric claim carries its evidence-log claim ID in brackets immediately after it, matching a real row in `data/evidence/evidence.csv`." House rule B1 says the same. I found six numbers that break this:

1. **§3.2, central "Gap" row**: "loses roughly **1.4 percentage points** of purchasing power a year" — no bracket ID. I recomputed it by hand: 3.1% (CASH-0016) − 1.65% (CASH-0014) = 1.45pp; the compounded real-return version, (1.0165/1.031)−1, is −1.406%. Either way, "1.4" is a defensible number — but it has no claim ID and no evidence.csv row.
2. **§3.2, high "Gap" row**: "lost roughly **9.7 percentage points** of purchasing power" — no bracket ID. Hand-check: 10.5% (CASH-0019) − 0.83% (CASH-0017) = 9.67pp, an exact match to the stated figure (the compounded version would be −8.75%, meaningfully different — see Finding 2). Correct as a simple subtraction, but again: no claim ID, no row.
3. **§4.1**: "the current Personal Allowance of **£12,570**" — quoted directly from gov.uk ("The standard Personal Allowance is £12,570") in the very same sentence, but not bracketed, and there is no CASH-#### row for it anywhere in evidence.csv (CASH-0028/0029/0030 cover the *band thresholds and rates*, not the Personal Allowance figure itself).
4. **§3.3**: "the only flexibility is a **30-day** cooling-off cancellation window" — no bracket ID, no row. I independently re-fetched NS&I's Guaranteed Growth Bonds page and confirmed the real quote exists ("you can cancel within 30 days of receiving confirmation of your Bond") — it's a genuine, citable fact that simply never got logged.
5. **§3.5**: "Typically **same working day to 1–2 business days**" for easy-access withdrawal — no bracket ID. This one is disclosed as unsourced in the same cell and listed in Open Questions §8.5, which is honest, but disclosure in prose doesn't substitute for a row (even a `sensitivity` row with blank source fields, per the README's row-type rules) — right now it's just a bare number with no log entry at all.
6. **§1**: "prizes ranging from £25 up to **£1 million**" — no bracket ID, no row. I independently confirmed via NS&I's own page: "Prizes range from £25 to £1 million and are all tax-free" — a real, quotable, easily-logged fact that was simply missed.

**Fix:** add a CASH-#### row for each of the six (items 1–2 can be `claim` or a derived-figure type with `calculation_ref` pointing to the component CASH-IDs they're built from, per the pattern `data/evidence/README.md` describes for calculated figures — a calculated figure still needs a row, it just cites "n/a — directly sourced/calculated from CASH-000X and CASH-000Y" rather than a fresh external source; items 3, 4 and 6 are trivial Tier A/B `claim` rows with real quotes I've already re-confirmed above; item 5 should either get a `sensitivity` row with blank source fields, per the unsourced-cost pattern, or be rewritten to strike the specific "1–2 business days" figure and say only "not independently sourced in this pass" without a bare number attached). Then bracket all six in the dossier text.

### 2. [Critical — needs Lucas resolution] NS&I's Tier B classification is argued reasonably but doesn't clearly clear methodology §7.1's bar, and seven `claim` rows depend on it

§1's tier reasoning treats NS&I as Tier B because it is "a government-owned, HM Treasury-backed body... and it publishes a specific, documented monthly method (an odds ratio and a prize fund rate, both set and disclosed each month) rather than an undocumented marketing claim."

I re-fetched NS&I's own Premium Bonds page specifically to check this claim. It does **not** publish a method for how the prize fund rate or odds are set — it publishes the *output* numbers only, with this exact line: "The prize fund rate, prize values, odds of winning and the way we allocate the number of prizes of each value are all variable, so they can change at any time." There's a link to a blog post ("How we share out Premium Bonds prizes") that might contain more, but the page the dossier cites and quotes from does not itself disclose a calculation method — it discloses a result, on a schedule. Methodology §7.1 says a seller's own source is "at most Tier C, unless it publishes its underlying data and method" — publishing a monthly number is not the same as publishing the method that produces it, and I don't think "government-owned, not a commercial seller" gets around the plain wording either, since §7.1's rule is written to catch "a source written by someone selling the asset" full stop, with no carve-out for public-sector sellers.

This isn't an academic quibble. If NS&I is actually Tier C, then **CASH-0001, 0002, 0003, 0020, 0025, 0026 and 0027** — seven `claim` rows, covering the prize fund rate, the odds, the tax-free status, the Guaranteed Growth Bonds rate, and the min/max Premium Bonds holding — would each rest on a single Tier C source with no `EXCEPTION APPROVED` marker, which `validate.py` (per `data/evidence/README.md`) rejects outright. (CASH-0021 is safer either way, since it already carries a genuine second, independent source — Skipton.)

To be clear: every one of these seven numbers is **factually accurate** — I independently re-confirmed all of them against NS&I's live pages and they match exactly. This is a tier-classification dispute, not an accuracy problem. But it's exactly the kind of "disagreement with the researcher" the house rules say escalates to Lucas if it survives a round, and given how much of the dossier's Premium Bonds and Guaranteed Growth Bonds content sits downstream of this one call, I'd rather flag it now than let it ride into round 2 unresolved. **Fix (pick one):** (a) Lucas confirms NS&I clears Tier B on some other stated basis (e.g., a public-sector-issuer carve-out doesn't currently exist in §7.1 — this would need to be added to the methodology, not just asserted in one dossier); (b) downgrade these seven rows to Tier C and either get `EXCEPTION APPROVED` from Lucas for each, or restructure them as corroborated (find a second source — e.g., a comparison site or the Treasury's own NS&I annual report — for at least the prize rate and odds) or as `sensitivity`/background where a headline doesn't strictly need them.

### 3. [Major] Gap in the CASH claim-ID sequence: CASH-0022 does not exist anywhere

`data/evidence/README.md` states claim numbers "are never reused — even if a claim is later withdrawn." The CASH sequence runs 0001–0021, then jumps straight to 0023, with no `withdrawn` row (or any row at all) for 0022, and no mention of it anywhere in the dossier or evidence.csv. Compare this to how `gold.md`'s evidence log actually handles a dropped figure: it leaves a `withdrawn` row in place (e.g. GOLD-0024, GOLD-0034, GOLD-0038) so the audit trail shows what happened to it. Here there's just a silent hole. It's very likely a claim was drafted and deleted during authoring rather than anything sinister, but as it stands there's no way to tell, and that's exactly the kind of gap that should have a `withdrawn` row explaining itself. **Fix:** either add a `withdrawn` row for CASH-0022 explaining what it was and why it was dropped, or confirm in writing (e.g. in the dossier's Open Questions) that the number was never assigned to a real claim and the gap is a clerical artefact.

### 4. [Minor] The inflation-drag "Gap" figures are arithmetically correct as defined, but the definition itself is never stated as a formula, only as prose

I recomputed both headline Gap figures by hand against the cited BoE/ONS inputs:
- Central: 3.1% (CASH-0016) − 1.65% (CASH-0014) = 1.45pp → dossier says "roughly 1.4." Close enough under "roughly," though 1.45 more naturally rounds to 1.5 than 1.4 — trivial, not worth blocking on.
- High: 10.5% (CASH-0019) − 0.83% (CASH-0017) = 9.67pp → dossier says "roughly 9.7." Exact match.

Both numbers check out as simple subtraction (nominal rate minus CPI), which is what §3.2's prose actually defines "Gap" as ("it is the gap between the interest rate paid and the rate of inflation (CPI)"). I flag only that this is a Fisher-approximation, not the compounded real-return formula methodology §1.2 uses elsewhere (which would give −8.75% for the high scenario, not −9.7% — a genuine ~1-point difference at high inflation, since simple subtraction increasingly overstates real erosion as inflation rises). The dossier's caveat that this "is not a formal Net Real Return figure" is adequate and I found no place where it's quietly walked back into being treated as one (Confidence and Risks sections both refer back to the raw rates, not to the derived percentage-point figures). **Fix (optional, for clarity rather than correctness):** state explicitly, once, that "Gap" = nominal rate minus CPI (a simplification), so a reader doesn't assume it's the same real-return arithmetic used elsewhere in this company's methodology.

### 5. [Minor] Two claim IDs are cited twice in a way that's redundant rather than wrong

§2 cites "£50,000 [CASH-0026] per-person maximum [CASH-0026]" — the same ID bracketed twice in one clause. Not a sourcing problem, just untidy; worth a copyedit pass.

### 6. What checked out cleanly (no fix needed)

- **Every one of the following quotes was independently re-fetched and matches the dossier word for word**: FSCS's "On 1 December 2025 the FSCS deposit protection rose to £120,000" and the £85,000/2017–2025 prior limit; BoE's July 2026 sight-deposit rate (1.65%) and December 2022 rate (0.83%); ONS's August 2026 CPI bulletin ("rose by 3.1%... up from 2.9%") and December 2022 bulletin ("rose by 10.5%... down from 10.7%"); gov.uk's Personal Savings Allowance figures (£1,000/£500/£0), starting-rate-for-savings threshold (£17,570) and amount (£5,000); gov.uk's Income Tax bands (20%/40%/45%, £12,571–£50,270, £50,271–£125,140, over £125,140) and the £12,570 Personal Allowance itself (see Finding 1.3 — accurate, just not logged); gov.uk's £20,000 ISA allowance; NS&I's Premium Bonds prize rate (4.35%) and odds (21,000 to 1), tax-free wording, min/max holding (£25/£50,000), and 3–5 day withdrawal timing; NS&I's Guaranteed Growth Bonds 1-year rate (4.82%) and "No, you cannot take money out until the Bond reaches the end of its term"; and Moneyfacts' current top advertised easy-access rate (5.00% AER, described as bonus-inclusive).
- **Every CASH-#### `source_1_url`/`source_2_url` I checked starts with `http` and points to a real, specific page** — no guessed or constructed homepage standing in for an unfetched page, and the two `sensitivity` rows (CASH-0023, CASH-0024) correctly leave every source field blank per the row-type rule.
- **Moneyfacts (Tier C, sole source for the "low" easy-access rate) is correctly logged as `row_type: sensitivity`, not `claim`** — this is the right call per the Tier-C-sole-support rule and matches the pattern `gold.md` uses for its own unsourced/Tier-C-only ranges (e.g. GOLD-0001–0003, GOLD-0040/41).
- **The fixed-rate-bond early-access-penalty sensitivity (CASH-0024, "up to 180 days' interest")** correctly follows methodology §3: excluded at low (£0, held to maturity) and central (total illiquidity, no fee concept applies), and only introduced as an explicitly labelled, unsourced upper-bound assumption at high — not dressed up as a verified figure.

- **All `last_verified` dates are 2026-09-21 (today)**, well within the 12-month validator threshold, and no `live` row exists yet needing `approved_by` — consistent with draft status.
