# Data licensing research: can we buy a licensed daily price/NAV series for the S&P 500 benchmark?

**Status:** research note, not a decision · **Owner:** researcher agent · **Date checked:** 2026-09-19
Built per `docs/methodology.md` (v0.8) and `docs/house-rules.md`. Where this note and those documents disagree, the documents win.

## Why this note exists

`dossiers/sp500.md` (section 1.1, section 5, Open Questions §8.10) and `dossiers/gold.md` both hit the same wall: the official index/benchmark provider (S&P Dow Jones Indices for the S&P 500; LBMA/ICE for gold) requires a paid commercial licence, and the fund issuers whose products this company actually benchmarks against (iShares CSPX, Vanguard VUSA/VUAG, Fidelity Index US Fund) don't offer a confirmed, downloadable, multi-year daily NAV history on their own retail websites — and even where they do, their website terms broadly restrict "derivative works."

This note asks a narrower, practical question: **is there a market-data vendor that (a) actually holds the daily price/NAV history we need, far enough back, and (b) whose licence terms explicitly say we may calculate a derived figure (a cost- and inflation-adjusted return statistic) from their data and publish that figure to a commercial audience** — not just use the raw data internally, not just display it as-is?

Four paid vendors were checked first: **EODHD, Twelve Data, Polygon.io (rebranded Massive.com, 30 Oct 2025), and Financial Modeling Prep (FMP)**. All four were reachable to some degree; FMP's own website blocked every direct-fetch attempt made in this pass (see its row below) so its entry is weaker than the other three and is flagged as such rather than treated as equivalent evidence.

**Added 2026-09-19, before any paid vendor is pursued further:** Lucas asked for two long-standing free academic datasets — Robert Shiller's (Yale) and Aswath Damodaran's (NYU Stern) — to be checked as well, since this company's actual need is narrower than a live commercial feed: month-end data points, the standard 1/5/10/20-year windows, and no need for current/live data. See the dedicated section below, after the paid-vendor comparison.

**Sourcing note on tiers:** per house rules, a vendor's own marketing or pricing page describing its own product is **Tier C** — background only, never sufficient on its own to support a number, because the vendor is the seller of what's being described. A vendor's own published **Terms of Service**, by contrast, is treated here as the **primary document for what the contract actually says** (there is no other source for that) — but it is still the seller's own document, drafted to protect the seller, so where it is ambiguous this note says so plainly rather than reading it generously. No number or licence conclusion below rests on a Tier C marketing claim alone.

## What this shows, read across all four vendors

Every vendor whose terms could actually be read in this pass — EODHD, Twelve Data, and Polygon.io/Massive — draws the same basic line, worded differently:

- Ordinary use of the data (building your own charts, running your own analysis) is fine under a standard paid plan.
- **Publishing something built from that data to people outside your own organisation is treated as a separate, more restricted thing** — at best gated behind an "add-on" or "separate written agreement" (Twelve Data), at worst banned outright without a specific licence naming "index," "net asset value" or "derivative works" (Polygon.io/Massive).
- None of the three readable vendors' published, self-serve pricing tiers come with a clause that plainly says: *"yes, you may calculate a return figure from our data and publish that figure to your paying customers or general audience."* Where the door is open at all (Twelve Data's Derived Data definition), it sits next to another clause that seems to require a separate, unpriced conversation before you can actually publish anything built from it.

This matches the pattern this company's own `dossiers/sp500.md` and `dossiers/gold.md` already found with S&P Dow Jones Indices, LBMA/ICE, iShares and Fidelity: **every source close to "the real number" treats redistribution and derivative-works rights as a separate, negotiated thing from basic data access**, not something a published price list settles on its own.

## Public research datasets, checked before any paid vendor: Shiller (Yale) and Damodaran (NYU Stern)

**Why this section exists.** Before spending any more time on paid vendors, Lucas asked for two long-standing free academic datasets to be checked, since this company's actual data need is narrower than "a live commercial feed": **month-end data points, not daily; the standard 1/5/10/20-year windows (methodology §4.1); no need for live/current data.** Both datasets were read directly and their licence/usage text quoted verbatim, per methodology §7.3. No bulk data file was downloaded in this pass — only each dataset's own web pages, describing what the file contains and on what terms.

**Sourcing note on tiers, consistent with the discipline used above:** each dataset's own page, describing its own contents and coverage, is treated the same way this note treats a fund issuer's own factsheet elsewhere in this company's dossiers — the primary, first-hand source for what it says, tiered **B** ("published... well-documented research," methodology §7.1), because both datasets are long-standing, widely cited in academic and practitioner finance work, and their own publisher explains sourcing and method. A third-party description or mirror of either dataset is **Tier C** unless it is itself an academic paper or an official body republishing the data. Licence/usage wording is quoted as the primary document for what it says, in the same way this note treats a vendor's Terms of Service — not generously or restrictively read where it is genuinely silent.

### Assessment against this company's specific needs

- **Frequency fit.** Shiller's data is **monthly**, which is exactly the granularity this company needs for month-end data points and for building the rolling-window analysis methodology §4.2 requires ("for each window length, every possible start month in the data"). Damodaran's S&P 500 series is **annual only** — it can support the standard fixed 1/5/10/20-year windows (methodology §4.1) if the window boundaries are set at calendar year-ends, but it **cannot** on its own satisfy §4.2's rolling, every-start-month requirement. This is a real, structural limitation of Damodaran's dataset for this company's primary benchmark use, not a licensing problem.
- **Depth and no live-data need.** Both comfortably exceed what is needed: Shiller's data runs from 1871, Damodaran's from 1928; this company needs at most a 20-year lookback from a fixed month-end, and neither dataset needs to be live or current-day, which matches the stated brief exactly. Neither vendor problem that dogged the four paid vendors above — "confirmed history depth far short of 20 years" (Massive.com's Indices tier; iShares' own CSPX download tool) — applies to either academic dataset.
- **Total return, already or in principle.** Damodaran's S&P 500 column is already stated to include dividends — the cleanest "yes" to the total-return question found in this whole research effort, paid or free. Shiller's file very likely contains an equivalent pre-built series (the page's own reference to "a total return version" points that way), but this is not confirmed to this company's citation standard without opening the file, which this pass deliberately did not do.
- **GBP and UK CPI — a gap common to both.** Neither dataset publishes GBP figures or UK CPI. Both are USD-priced and, where CPI is included at all (Shiller only), it is **US** CPI, not the ONS CPI methodology §7.2 requires for this company's real-return calculations. Using either dataset for this company's benchmark would still require pairing it with a separate GBP/USD exchange-rate series (e.g. Bank of England's own published rates, Tier A, not checked in this pass) and, for a "real" GBP figure, ONS CPI instead of (or alongside) any US CPI series embedded in the data. This is not a flaw unique to either dataset — the same gap would exist for any USD-denominated index series, including a licensed one from a paid vendor.
- **Licence fit for a published, commercial derived figure.** This is where the two datasets diverge sharply. **Damodaran's** explicit "I want the data to be widely used" framing, with only two named and irrelevant-to-this-company exclusions (court cases, public policy debates), reads far more permissively than any paid vendor's Terms of Service found in this document — nothing in it suggests publishing a sourced, attributed derived figure in commercial video, newsletter or app content would be a problem, though it is informal prose, not a drafted legal grant. **Shiller's** page, by contrast, is genuinely silent: it grants no explicit right to redistribute, cite, or build derivative works, but nor does it prohibit doing so the way EODHD's, Twelve Data's or Polygon.io/Massive's ToS explicitly do above. Per this note's stated rule, that silence is not read as permission — it is recorded as an open question.
- **Item- vs index-type sourcing and survivorship.** Both datasets report the same underlying "S&P 500," so they inherit the same index-level, committee-based survivorship characteristics already documented in `dossiers/sp500.md` section 6 (weaker/failed constituents are dropped, not carried at near-zero value). This is not a distinguishing factor between the two datasets, or against S&P DJI's own index — it is a property of the S&P 500 index construction itself, however the data reaches this company.

## Recommendation

**No vendor checked in this pass can be recommended for a subscription today**, on the evidence gathered:

1. **Twelve Data** is the strongest lead: it is the only vendor that directly confirmed holding the exact UK tickers this company already uses (CSPX and VUSA, matched to the LSE/GBP listing used in `dossiers/sp500.md`), and its Terms of Use are the only ones that name a "Derived Data" concept that sounds like what a Net Real Return calculation actually is. But its own Terms do not clearly say whether publishing that Derived Data to a public or paid audience needs the separate "Redistribution Rights Add-On" — and that add-on has no published price, meaning the real cost of a workable Twelve Data licence is unknown until Twelve Data's sales team answers a direct question.
2. **EODHD** is plausible on coverage (confirmed LSE access; S&P 500 index ticker referenced in its own documentation, though not confirmed on a pricing page) but equally unclear on licensing, and — like Twelve Data — points unresolved cases to its sales team rather than answering in its published terms.
3. **Polygon.io/Massive** should be ruled out now: it has no UK exchange coverage at all, so it cannot supply the ETF/NAV side of what's needed regardless of price, and separately, its own terms are the most explicit and specific prohibition on exactly this use found anywhere in this research (naming "index," "net asset value" and "investment strategy" as things you may not derive from its data without a specific licence).

### Option A — ONS's own "not National Statistics" modelled CPI/CPIH estimates, 1950 to 1988

Fetched directly from `https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/datasets/consumerpriceindices` (ONS's "Consumer price inflation time series" dataset page — the same family of dataset the benchmark's D7BT series comes from), 2026-09-21. The page states, quoted verbatim:

> "CPIH and CPI estimates for the period 1950 to 1988 (1949 to 1987 for index values) are also available."

and, immediately qualifying that:

> "Please note that these earlier estimates are not National Statistics. They have been modelled and are provided purely for indicative purposes."

The same page separately describes a different, later extension:

> "In December 2018, we published extended series for the components of CPIH covering 1988 to 2004 and CPI covering 1988 to 1995."

> "All of the extended series were modelled after the introduction of CPIH and CPI and do not form part of the ongoing National Statistics series at 1 decimal place."

The page's licence statement, quoted verbatim: **"All content is available under the Open Government Licence v3.0, except where otherwise stated."**

**Assessment against the task's five points:**

1. **What it is / publisher:** ONS itself publishes it, inside the same downloadable dataset (MM23-family) that already supplies this company's D7BT series — it is not a separate product from a different body.
2. **How far back:** to 1950 (1949 for index values) — 38 years further back than D7BT's 1988 start, but still 22 years short of Damodaran's 1928 start.
3. **Real measurement, official retrospective estimate, or composite/spliced series:** ONS's own words settle this: **"modelled"** and **"not National Statistics."** This is explicitly a retrospective estimate, not a live measurement taken at the time, and ONS itself distinguishes it from the National Statistics-badged CPI series the benchmark currently uses. This is exactly the kind of methodological break methodology §7.2's "CPI throughout" principle is trying to avoid, and ONS is saying so about its own data, not something this research is inferring.
4. **Licence:** Open Government Licence v3.0 (the page's blanket statement; no override text was seen for this specific dataset) — the same licence D7BT itself uses. **Tier:** this needs a careful call. It is published by ONS (an official body, normally Tier A per methodology §7.1), but ONS's own label — "not National Statistics," "modelled," "indicative" — is a direct statement that this specific series does not carry the same evidential weight as ONS's official statistics. This note treats it as **Tier B** ("well-documented research," published by ONS but explicitly not standing as official statistics), not Tier A, and flags that distinction for Lucas since it affects whether this series could support a headline number on its own under methodology §7.1's rule ("Tier A: can support a headline on its own").
5. **Downloadable / usable at annual frequency:** Described as available within the same ONS consumer price inflation dataset page already used for D7BT — the page states the estimates "are also available" but does not show a separate CDID/series code or a distinct file link that this pass could isolate and quote; the exact file/tab containing the 1950–1988 figures was not opened (per the task's instruction not to download bulk data). This is a genuine gap: the series' *existence* and *licence* are confirmed, but its *exact downloadable location and format* is not yet pinned down.

### Option B — ONS's Retail Prices Index (RPI), a different, longer-running measure

Fetched directly from `https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/timeseries/chaw/mm23` (CDID **CHAW**, "RPI All Items Index: Jan 1987=100"), 2026-09-21. The page states this specific series' own displayed earliest date is **January 1987** and its licence, quoted verbatim: **"All content is available under the Open Government Licence v3.0, except where otherwise stated."**

RPI as a published measure is widely known to run monthly back to 1947 (linking an "Interim Index of Retail Prices" from 1947 to the modern RPI), but **this session could not confirm that longer depth first-hand**: the specific CDID/page fetched showed only the post-1987-rebasing display, and a direct question to the page about whether it links to pre-1987 data returned no confirmation either way. This is recorded as unconfirmed, not asserted.

**Assessment:**

1. **What it is / publisher:** ONS's Retail Prices Index — a different index from CPI, using arithmetic rather than CPI's geometric averaging and a different basket, which is why RPI and CPI give different inflation readings for the same period even when both exist.
2. **How far back:** genuinely uncertain within this pass — CDID CHAW's own page shows January 1987 as its earliest displayed point; whether ONS still separately publishes the pre-1987 linked series in a form fetchable at the same URL family was not established.
3. **Real measurement vs. estimate:** RPI itself, where it exists, is understood to be a real contemporaneous measurement (unlike Option A's admitted "modelled" 1950–1988 CPI estimates) — but this is background knowledge, not confirmed by a fetched ONS page in this pass, so it is not cited as settled.
4. **Licence:** OGL v3.0, per the page fetched. **Tier A**, if the depth-of-history question above is resolved — RPI is still an ONS official statistic (unlike Option A), just a different index from CPI.
5. **Downloadable:** yes, in the same ONS timeseries format already used for D7BT — no new technical barrier.

**The bigger issue with Option B is not sourcing, it's methodology.** RPI is not CPI. Splicing "RPI pre-1988, CPI 1988-onward" would not be "the same measure throughout" — it would be two different, methodologically distinct indices joined at a seam, which is precisely the kind of change methodology §7.2 currently forecloses by naming CPI specifically. See the flag at the end of this section.

LSEG may also at times be used as source."** and, under "Copyright / disclaimer": **"©2025 LSEG. Republication or redistribution of LSEG content, including by framing or similar means, is prohibited without the prior written consent of LSEG."** — **Tier A**, logged as `SP500-0074`.

This page names the "spot exchange rates" data category as a whole, not XUDLGBD individually — a reasonable, disclosed inference given it is the exact page the Bank's own database links from the XUDLGBD results screen, not an independent guess. It settles the question the Bank's general legal page left open: XUDLGBD's series family is one of the third-party-licensed exclusions, sourced from Bloomberg BFIX and/or LSEG, and is genuinely not freely redistributable under the OGL.

**What this changes, and what it doesn't:**
- This company has only ever used XUDLGBD to compute its own derived annual return figures, never republished the raw daily rates themselves — so nothing in `costs/benchmark/` needs to change to stay within LSEG's restriction.
- The remaining question is a policy one for Lucas, not a factual one: whether to keep building published figures on LSEG/Bloomberg-sourced data under this internal-use-only basis indefinitely, or to seek a proper data licence, or switch to an unambiguously open FX source, before this benchmark (or anything built on it) is published externally. Tracked in `docs/open-research-queue.md`.
- This does **not** resolve the separate, still-open question about whether the same OGL statement covers the Bank's distinct "research datasets" section (Option C above, the millennium macroeconomic dataset) — that is a different product on the Bank's site and was not addressed by this fetch.
